# E4 plan. Repeat Previous, lags unknown

Written 2026-09-28. Status before pilot. The frozen plan is `e4/plan.json` once the pilot gate is decided.

## Purpose

E4 repeats the demand-shift design of E1 on POPGym's Repeat Previous, using the official package, with no teacher. Training poses one lag. Deployment poses lags nobody trained for. The prediction is the E1 crossover. Regret on the known lag picks memories that keep what that lag needs. Freedom under an ignorance prior over lags picks memories that keep the window, and wins on the lags nobody trained for.

Two things make it stronger than E1. The environment is a public memory benchmark, installed from `pip`, not re-implemented. The correct answer at every step is a function of the observations, so no teacher exists and the teacher objection does not arise.

## Environment

POPGym `RepeatPrevious(num_decks=1, k)` from `popgym==1.0.7` (`popgym.envs.repeat_previous`). `RepeatPreviousEasy` is `num_decks=1, k=4`.

- A deck of 52 cards, 13 of each suit, is shuffled without replacement. The observation at card $t$ is its suit $o_t\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. The reset returns $o_1$, and each step returns the next card, so the episode has 51 steps and the agent acts at steps $t=1,\dots,51$ having seen $o_1,\dots,o_t$.
- The correct action at step $t$ under lag $k$ is $o_{t-k+1}$, defined for $t\ge k$. I verified this against the package on seeded episodes. Use this verified rule rather than the docstring's wording. Lag 1 is the current card.
- Reward at step $t\ge k$ is $+1/(52-k)$ for the correct suit and $-1/(52-k)$ otherwise, and $0$ for $t<k$. The return lies in $[-1,1]$ and a perfect agent scores $1$.
- Actions never change observations, so one panel of histories serves every candidate, as in E1.

Agents receive the one-hot suit (4 inputs). No step counter, no extra flags.

Source and target episodes have the same distribution. Only the question changes. The source lag is $k_{\mathrm{src}}=4$. The ignorance prior is uniform over every question an episode can pose, lag $k$ at step $t$ for every $k\le t$, as Section 4.2 of the paper defines it. Over lags 1 to 51 this weights lag $k$ by $52-k$, and 6 of the 51 lags are deployed. Deployment poses the new lags $\{2,3,5,6,7,8\}$, and the known lag 4 is reported beside them.

### Port and its check

Training needs batches. Write `e4/rprev.py`, a batched NumPy port that takes a shuffled suit order per episode and produces observations, labels for every lag, and rewards. Validate it once per world against the official environment. For 200 seeded episodes, run `RepeatPrevious(1, 4)` with a fixed action sequence, read the dealt order from `env.deck`, feed the same order to the port, and assert identical observations, rewards and labels for lags 1 to 16. Record `port_check: true` and the popgym version in every lock. Fresh target episodes are generated by the official environment class, seeded from the lock, and their suit order is passed to the port only to compute labels.

## Candidates

Nine architectures from `e2/agent.py`, with one change. The transformer takes one block as an option.

| Architecture | Sizes | What it can keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GRU | width 8, 32, 128 | learned |
| LSTM | width 8, 32, 128 | learned |
| Gated transformer, 2 blocks, 32-dim, 2 heads | attention window 8, 32 | receptive field $2w-1$, so 15 and 63 steps |
| Gated transformer, 1 block, 32-dim, 2 heads | attention window 4 | lags 1 to 4 only |

Two training seeds per architecture and snapshots at updates 150 and 300 give 36 candidates per world. The memory is the policy head's input at step $t$, as in E2. The one-block window-4 transformer is in the pool on purpose. Its receptive field is 4 steps, so it answers lag 4 and cannot answer lag 5. Two blocks of window 4 would reach 7 steps. The lock tags its four candidates per world as `win4`, and every confirmatory test also runs with them excluded.

Training is PPO from `e2/ppo.py` with the E2 settings. 300 updates of 64 episodes, three epochs, minibatches of 32, learning rate $10^{-3}$, entropy $0.01$, discount $0.99$, generalised advantage $0.95$, clipping $0.2$, value coefficient $0.5$, gradient norm $0.5$. Four actions. The pilot may cut updates to 200 if every architecture converges by 150.

Admission. A candidate is admitted when its native return on 512 held-out source episodes is within $0.10$ of the best candidate in its world. Source data only. If the pilot shows every candidate above $0.95$, the frozen plan sets the margin to $0.05$.

## Selectors

Every selector is computed in the source stage on the candidate's memories over a shared panel of 512 target episodes. The panel carries no rewards. Labels for any lag are computed from the panel's observations, which is what an agent ignorant of the question can do. The deployed lag set is never used by a selector, only by the reference `k_deploy`, which is stored and flagged.

For each lag $k\in\{1,\dots,51\}$, $d_k$ is the cross-fitted error of a multinomial logistic readout from the memory at step $t$ to $o_{t-k+1}$, over steps $t\ge k$, unit weights, five folds grouped by episode, $\ell_2$ penalty $10^{-3}$ on standardised memories. This is the linear-language deficit of Theorem 4.2 with the multi-class reduction of Theorem A.3. Weights are all one because the answer is a function of the history, so success weights and unit weights coincide. Add a four-class fit to `common/fsel.py` beside the binary one.

| Selector | Definition | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `k_known` | $d_4$, minimise | Regret on the known question |
| `f_ign` | mean of $d_1,\dots,d_{51}$ weighted by $52-k$, minimise | Proposed, freedom under the ignorance prior |
| `f_ign16` | mean of $d_1,\dots,d_{16}$, minimise | Exploratory, prior on lags 1 to 16 |
| `f_ign8` | mean of $d_1,\dots,d_8$, minimise | Exploratory, prior on lags 1 to 8 |
| `k_deploy` | mean of $d_k$ over the deployed new lags, minimise | Reference, knows the question list |
| `val` | native return on 512 held-out source episodes, maximise | Source validation |
| `skd` | $d_4$ on the source episodes, minimise | Source deficit |
| `mp`, `ent`, `atc`, `dis`, `dev` | as in E1, on the known lag | Label-free rivals |
| `params` | parameter count, maximise | Naive rival, pick the biggest |

A selector that ties candidates within $10^{-9}$ deploys each of them with equal probability, and its endpoint is the mean over the tied set. The candidate index follows architecture and size, so a lowest-index rule would favour the smallest memories. Record, per world, whether the two best candidates under `k_known` lie within $0.005$, because the known lag may not separate candidates that all answer it.

Refit readouts. For every lag 1 to 16 fit the multinomial logistic readout on the full panel and store its weights in the lock. Deployment uses them.

## Stages

The protocol of Section 7.1 of the paper, unchanged. Source stage per world, opening record after all 32 locks, then the target stage. No world is kept, dropped or rerun on a selector's behaviour or a target outcome. Every lock records the fresh seed, the popgym version, the port check and the flag that the deployed lag set was used by no selector.

Target stage. 1000 fresh episodes from the official environment per world. For each candidate and each lag $k\in\{1,\dots,16\}$, the refit success $s_k$ is the share of steps $t\ge k$ where the stored readout's answer equals $o_{t-k+1}$. The native return at lag 4 on the same episodes is `n_known`.

## Endpoints

- `l_new`, the mean of $s_k$ over the six new lags. Primary.
- `l_beyond`, the mean over lags 5 to 8. `l_win`, the mean over lags 2 and 3. `l_known`, $s_4$. `n_known`. Secondary.
- Gap closure between the admitted-pool mean and the target oracle on `l_new`, as a ratio of sums over worlds, with 50,000 bootstrap resamples. Tail rate, the share of worlds where a selector deploys a bottom-quartile candidate.
- Per-lag curves $s_1,\dots,s_{16}$ of the candidates each selector deployed, averaged over worlds. This is the mechanism figure.

## Hypotheses

Confirmatory family, Holm-corrected together, one-sided sign-flip tests at $\alpha=0.05$, one million random flips for 32 worlds. Every confirmatory test runs twice, on the full admitted pool and on the pool with the four window-4 transformer candidates removed, so the crossover is tested with and without a memory built to keep only the known lag. The family holds all sixteen tests and both results go in the paper.

- H1. `f_ign` beats `k_known` on `l_new`. Beside it, `k_known` beats or ties `f_ign` on `l_known`, and the crossover, the sum of the two paired differences, gets a bootstrap interval.
- H2. `f_ign` beats `val`, `mp`, `ent`, `atc`, `dis`, `dev` and `params` on `l_new`, one paired test per rival.

Exploratory, reported without correction.

- H3. Calibration. Within worlds, `l_new` regressed on $1-$ `k_deploy` has slope near one.
- H4. `l_beyond` separates the selectors more than `l_win`.
- H5. `f_ign8` and `f_ign16` against `f_ign` on `l_new`.
- H6. The per-lag curves. The memories `k_known` deploys should fall off after lag 4 and the memories `f_ign` deploys should not.
- H7. Share of worlds in which `k_known` deploys the window-4 transformer.

Interpretation fixed in advance. If `f_ign` and `k_known` deploy the same candidate in most worlds and H1 fails, the finding is that capacity did not bind, the pool kept the window whatever it was trained on, and the paper reports it as a null result beside E1.

## Worlds, seeds and statistics

32 worlds. World seed is $1100000+1021\cdot\mathrm{wid}$, disjoint from E1 (500000), E2 (700000) and E3 (900000). Pilot seeds 1100000 minus 1021 and minus 2042. Each world has its own training seeds, held-out source episodes, panel and fresh episodes. The world is the unit and every comparison is paired within world. Bootstrap 50,000. Holm within the confirmatory family.

## Pilot and gates

Two pilot worlds on the pilot seeds, full pool, full pipeline including the target stage. Record `e4/pilot.json`. Gates, checked in order.

- G1. At least a quarter of candidates reach source return $0.9$. Fails, double the updates to 600 and rerun the pilot.
- G2. Mean within-world spread of `l_new` across admitted candidates, best minus worst, at least $0.15$. Fails, extend the new lags to $\{2,3,6,8,10,12\}$, and rerun. Fails again, replace the second training seed by an auxiliary head that predicts the suits of the last eight cards with weight $0.5$, off or on, as in E1, and rerun.

Freeze `e4/plan.json` with the surviving settings, the pilot numbers and the gate outcomes before the first confirmatory world runs.

## Compute and code

Cost. 36 candidates in 32 worlds is 1152 PPO runs of about one million steps. E2 ran a comparable candidate in about two minutes on one core of the Mac, so expect 20 to 60 core-hours, 6 to 15 hours on the Mac's four cores. The source stage after training is a few minutes per world. The target stage is seconds. The deficits need about 9,000 multinomial fits per world, 51 lags for 36 candidates in five folds, so the pilot times them. No GPU is needed. Width 128 recurrent cells at batch 64 run as fast on a CPU core, and only the window-32 transformer might gain from one. The pilot timing decides.
